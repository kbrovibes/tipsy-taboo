What it is

A deck of Taboo cards that lives in your pocket

The box game is great, and the box game is a nuisance. Half the cards are bent, the buzzer needs a battery nobody has, and the timer went missing in 2019. Meanwhile everybody at the table is already holding a phone.

Tipsy Taboo is that deck without the box. One phone is the deck, the clock, the scoreboard and the buzzer. Pick teams, pick a difficulty, pass it to whoever is giving clues. Then there are only two things you can do with a card: swipe right when they say it, or swipe left to pass.

Pass covers both of the ways a card dies. You are stuck and want the next one — or a banned word slipped out and the card is void. At the table those are the same moment, so they are the same button. The house rules decide how many passes are free before they start costing you.

Got it · +1

Swipe right, or hit the green button. They said the word. One point on the board.

Pass · 3 free

Swipe left, or hit the left button. Stuck, or a taboo word slipped out — either way the card is gone. Free while your passes last.

Pass · −1

Out of free passes, the same button turns red and costs a point. Every pass after that comes off your score.

When the clock runs out, every card of the turn is listed — each pass that cost you carries a −1 badge — so a mis-swipe can be fixed before the points are banked.

Who it’s for

  • A table with one charged phone. Two teams is a game, four is a riot. Nobody else needs to install anything.
  • Families and mixed ages. The Kids deck is animals, snacks and cartoons; Insane is for people who think Hard is cute.
  • Desi households and diaspora nights. Eight themed packs, from Bollywood to Doordarshan-era 90s India.
  • Anyone who plays in the dark. Light, dark and auto, and auto is the default — the app just follows the phone.
  • Not for stat-keepers. No accounts, no leaderboards, no history. When the night ends, so does the game.

Setting up

Teams, deck, clock

Two to four teams, named by them or by the app. Then a deck, a turn length, a round count, and how many passes come free.

  • Kids, Medium, Hard and Insane
  • Eight themed packs plus The Big Mix
  • 60, 90 or 120 seconds a turn
  • 3, 5, 8 rounds, or endless
  • Free passes: none, 3, or unlimited

The handoff

Pass it to the clue-giver

Between turns the screen shows nothing but whose go it is, so the phone can cross the table without leaking the next card. The clue-giver taps start, gets a three-two-one countdown, and the deck deals.

  • No card is on screen until the clock starts
  • Scores are visible to everyone between turns
  • The screen stays awake for the whole turn

The turn

Say it, or pass

The word sits above the five you cannot say. Swipe right when the table shouts it. Swipe left if you are stuck — or if one of the five just came out of your mouth, because that is the same card and the same button.

  • Got it +1 · pass 0 while free ones remain
  • Then the pass button turns red: −1 each
  • Free passes per turn: none, 3, or unlimited
  • Sounds, haptics, and arrow keys on a laptop

The referee link

A second phone as the buzzer

Optional, and worth it. Open /watch on another phone, type the four letters from the game menu or scan the QR, and the other team gets the live card and one enormous TABOO! button.

  • TABOO! passes the card on the host phone
  • Same single action — no third rule to remember
  • Mirrors the card, the clock and the scores
  • Nothing is stored: a broadcast and no more

After the buzzer

Argue about it, then bank it

Time’s up and every card comes back in a list with what it scored, a −1 badge on the passes that cost. Someone swiped the wrong way in the panic? Tap it and flip it. The points go on the board only when the team is happy.

  • Fix a mis-swipe before it counts
  • Every team plays once per round
  • A tie at the end adds one more round

Appearance

Light, dark, or auto

Auto is the default, so the app is whatever the phone already is — and flips with it when the sun goes down. Pin it to light or dark from the menu if the room disagrees with the phone.

  • Auto follows the device, no setting needed
  • Same lime and grape accents in both
  • Readable at arm’s length across the table

How it works

From “shall we play something” to a running game

Four steps, and only one of them involves the person who owns the phone.

01

Split the table

Two to four teams, sat so each can glare at the other. Name them, or let the app do it.

02

Set the house rules

A deck, a turn length, a round count and a free-pass allowance. The defaults are Hard, 60 seconds, 5 rounds, 3 free passes.

03

Pass the phone

The clue-giver taps start and talks fast. Their team shouts; the other team listens for one of the five.

04

Settle up

Fix any mis-swipes on the recap, bank the points, hand the phone on. Highest score after the last round wins.

The cards

How 6,280 cards got written

A card is a word and the five words you may not use for it. That second part is the whole game, and the easy part to get lazily wrong.

The rule that makes a card good

Ban the five words a person actually reaches for first, not five that happen to be related. Say the target out loud in one breath and listen to what comes out — those five are the ban list. Hard, and still winnable.

Get them to say

Elephant

  • Trunk
  • Grey
  • Tusk
  • Africa
  • Big

Now describe it. That is the game.

Written by a team of Claude sub-agents

The cards were written in parallel by Claude sub-agents working against a written spec, then run through a validator that throws out anything breaking it. Nothing shipped that a machine could not prove was legal.

  1. Exactly five bans, all different, all one to three words.
  2. A stem-collision check. A ban may not contain the target, any word of it, or a four-letter stem — so “Swimming” can never ban “Swimmer”.
  3. No repeat targets inside a deck, spelling variants included.
  4. Easiest tier wins. If a word landed in two decks, it stays in the easier one and leaves the harder one.
  5. Family-friendly throughout, and stricter still on the Kids deck.

The spec and the build script are in the repo, at data/SPEC.md and scripts/build-decks.mjs.

Each pack runs from 240 to 346 cards. Every deck is dealt like a real one: shuffled, then drawn from front to back. This phone remembers which cards it has already played, per deck and across games, so a second round tonight cannot hand you the same word twice.

Features

What’s actually in it

Two-action scoring

Right for got it, left to pass, and the whole turn scores itself. No pencil, no scrap of paper, no arguments about the total.

Free passes, then a price

Neutral while your allowance holds, red once it runs out. None, three or unlimited — the table decides how forgiving the night is.

Recap before it counts

Every card played in the turn, listed with its outcome and a −1 badge on the passes that cost. Tap to flip. Points bank only when the team says so.

Referee link

Four letters or a QR code puts the live card on a second phone, with one big TABOO! button that passes it. Optional, instant, nothing written down.

Light, dark, auto

Auto by default, so it matches the phone and flips with it. Pin it either way from the menu when the room disagrees.

Survives everything

A wake lock holds the display on through a turn. Lock the phone anyway, take a call, refresh the tab — the game and the scores are still there.

Sound and feel

A tiny synth built in the browser makes the ticks, the dings and the buzz. Haptics on every swipe. Both can be turned off.

Installs, then goes offline

Add it to the home screen and it runs full screen with no browser chrome, on a train, in a garden, with no signal at all.

Keyboard on a laptop

Arrow keys score, space pauses. Playing off a laptop on the coffee table works exactly as well.

Privacy

Where nothing goes

The game runs entirely in the browser. Teams, scores, house rules, your appearance choice and the list of cards this phone has seen live in localStorage and are never sent anywhere. The referee link is the one exception: a Supabase Realtime broadcast channel that mirrors the card to a second phone and carries a TABOO! back, storing nothing at either end. No accounts, no ad network, no analytics, no cookie banner — there are no cookies to warn you about.

FAQ

Reasonable questions

Is it free?

Yes, with no ads and nothing to unlock. It is a static app on Vercel’s free tier, and the referee channel is idle unless somebody uses it.

Someone said a taboo word. Now what?

Swipe left. That is it — the pass button covers both being stuck and letting a banned word out, because in both cases the card is dead. It is free while the team’s free passes last and costs a point after that. Derived forms count: if “Swim” is banned, so is “swimming”.

Why did you drop the separate buzz button?

Because at the table it was never a separate moment. Somebody says a banned word, somebody yells, and the card is over — which is exactly what a pass already does. One button is faster, and one fewer rule to explain to the person who arrived late.

How many phones do I need?

One. A second phone is nice for the referee link, so the other team gets its own TABOO! button, but the game is complete on a single device.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, after the first visit. Add it to your home screen and the decks, the sounds and the game are cached. Only the referee link needs a connection.

What happens to my data?

Nothing leaves the phone. Team names, scores and the record of cards played sit in your browser’s own storage. The only thing that goes over the wire is a referee broadcast, stored at neither end.

Can kids play?

The Kids deck is 1,102 cards of animals, snacks, cartoons and school things, written for six to nine year olds. Every deck is family-friendly; Kids is stricter again.

Can I make it dark?

It already is, if your phone is. Appearance is light, dark or auto, and auto is the default. Pin it either way from the menu.
