What it is

A deck of Taboo cards that lives in your pocket

The box game is great, and the box game is a nuisance. Half the cards are bent, the buzzer needs a battery nobody has, and the timer went missing in 2019. Meanwhile everybody at the table is already holding a phone.

Tipsy Taboo is that deck without the box. One phone is the deck, the clock, the scoreboard and the buzzer. Pick teams, pick a difficulty, pass it to whoever is giving clues. Swipe right when they get it, swipe left to skip, and tap the red hand when a banned word slips out — that last one is the other team’s button, and it costs a point.

When the clock runs out, every card of the turn is listed so a mis-swipe can be fixed before the points are banked.

Who it’s for

  • A table with one charged phone. Two teams is a game, four is a riot. Nobody else needs to install anything.
  • Families and mixed ages. The Kids deck is animals, snacks and cartoons; Insane is for people who think Hard is cute.
  • Desi households and diaspora nights. Eight themed packs, from Bollywood to Doordarshan-era 90s India.
  • Not for stat-keepers. No accounts, no leaderboards, no history. When the night ends, so does the game.

Setting up

Teams, deck, clock

Two to four teams, named by them or by the app. Then a deck, a turn length, and however many rounds you have the stamina for.

  • Kids, Medium, Hard and Insane
  • Eight themed packs plus The Big Mix
  • 60, 90 or 120 seconds a turn
  • 3, 5, 8 rounds, or endless

The handoff

Pass it to the clue-giver

Between turns the screen shows nothing but whose go it is, so the phone can cross the table without leaking the next card. The clue-giver taps start, gets a three-two-one countdown, and the deck deals.

  • No card is on screen until the clock starts
  • Scores are visible to everyone between turns
  • The screen stays awake for the whole turn

The turn

Swipe it, skip it, or get buzzed

The word sits above the five you cannot say. Swipe right when the table shouts it, left to bin it — and talk carefully, because the other team’s finger is on the red hand.

  • Got it +1 · skipped 0 · buzzed −1
  • Skips capped at 0, 3, or let loose
  • Sounds, haptics, and arrow keys on a laptop

The referee link

A second phone as the buzzer

Optional, and worth it. Open /watch on another phone, type the four letters from the game menu or scan the QR, and the other team gets the live card and one enormous BUZZ button.

  • Four-letter code, or a QR from the menu
  • Mirrors the card, the clock and the scores
  • Nothing is stored: a broadcast and no more

After the buzzer

Argue about it, then bank it

Every card comes back in a list with what it scored. Someone swiped the wrong way in the panic? Tap it and flip it. The points go on the board only when the team is happy.

  • Fix a mis-swipe before it counts
  • Every team plays once per round
  • A tie at the end adds one more round

How it works

From “shall we play something” to a running game

Four steps, and only one of them involves the person who owns the phone.

01

Split the table

Two to four teams, sat so each can glare at the other. Name them, or let the app do it.

02

Set the house rules

A deck, a turn length, a round count and a skip limit. The defaults are Hard, 60 seconds, 5 rounds, 3 skips.

03

Pass the phone

The clue-giver taps start and talks fast. Their team shouts; the other team watches for a banned word.

04

Settle up

Fix any mis-swipes on the recap, bank the points, hand the phone on. Highest score after the last round wins.

The cards

How thousands of cards got written

A card is a word and the five words you may not use for it. That second part is the whole game, and the easy part to get lazily wrong.

The rule that makes a card good

Ban the five words a person actually reaches for first, not five that happen to be related. Say the target out loud in one breath and listen to what comes out — those five are the ban list. Hard, and still winnable.

Get them to say

Elephant

  • Trunk
  • Grey
  • Tusk
  • Africa
  • Big

Now describe it. That is the game.

Written by a team of Claude sub-agents

Every card was generated against a written spec, then run through a validator that throws out anything breaking it. Nothing shipped that a machine could not prove was legal.

  1. Exactly five bans, all different, all one to three words.
  2. A stem-collision check. A ban may not contain the target, any word of it, or a four-letter stem — so “Swimming” can never ban “Swimmer”.
  3. No repeat targets inside a deck, spelling variants included.
  4. Easiest tier wins. If a word landed in two decks, it stays in the easier one and leaves the harder one.
  5. Family-friendly throughout, and stricter still on the Kids deck.

The spec and the build script are in the repo, at data/SPEC.md and scripts/build-decks.mjs.

Four levels

🧸 Kids

Animals, snacks and cartoons. For six to nine year olds and the adults humouring them.

🍹 Medium

Classic party-pack cards — everyday words the whole table already knows.

🌶️ Hard

Idioms, feelings, general knowledge. The most-played level: thirty seconds of squirming per card.

💀 Insane

Jargon, deep cuts and named effects. Brutal but fair, for people who think Hard is cute.

Features

What’s actually in it

Swipe scoring

Right for got it, left to skip, and the whole turn scores itself. No pencil, no scrap of paper, no arguments about the total.

A buzzer that works

A red hand on the guessing screen, or a full-screen BUZZ button on a second phone. It sounds, it shakes, it costs a point.

Recap before it counts

Every card played in the turn, listed with its outcome and tappable to flip. Points bank only when the team says so.

Referee link

Four letters or a QR code puts the live card on a second phone. Optional, instant, and nothing is written down anywhere.

Survives everything

A wake lock holds the display on through a turn. Lock the phone anyway, take a call, refresh the tab — the game and the scores are still there.

Sound and feel

A tiny synth built in the browser makes the ticks, the dings and the buzz. Haptics on every swipe. Both can be turned off.

Installs, then goes offline

Add it to the home screen and it runs full screen with no browser chrome, on a train, in a garden, with no signal at all.

Keyboard on a laptop

Arrow keys score, space pauses, B buzzes. Playing off a laptop on the coffee table works exactly as well.

The game runs entirely in the browser. Teams, scores, settings and the list of cards this phone has seen live in localStorage and are never sent anywhere. The referee link is the one exception: a Supabase Realtime broadcast channel that mirrors the card to a second phone and carries a buzz back, storing nothing at either end. No accounts, no ad network, no analytics.

FAQ

Reasonable questions

Is it free?

Yes, with no ads and nothing to unlock. It is a static app on Vercel’s free tier, and the referee channel is idle unless somebody uses it.

How many phones do I need?

One. A second phone is nice for the referee link so the other team has its own buzz button, but the game is complete on a single device.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, after the first visit. Add it to your home screen and the decks, the sounds and the game are cached. Only the referee link needs a connection.

What happens to my data?

Nothing leaves the phone. Team names, scores and the record of cards played sit in your browser’s own storage. The only thing that goes over the wire is a referee broadcast, stored at neither end.

Can kids play?

The Kids deck is animals, snacks, cartoons and school things, written for six to nine year olds. Every deck is family-friendly; Kids is stricter again.

Someone said a taboo word. Now what?

The other team taps the red hand. It buzzes, the card is binned, a point comes off. Derived forms count: if “Swim” is banned, so is “swimming”.
